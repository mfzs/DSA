The Lamp in the Rain
On a quiet evening in a small village, the rain began to fall softly. Most people hurried indoors, closing
their doors and windows. But at the end of a narrow street stood an old shop with a single warm lamp
glowing in the window.
The shop belonged to Mr. Arun, a gentle old man who fixed broken things. Clocks, radios, toys, even
old umbrellas—people brought him anything that no longer worked.
One night, a young boy named Rohan ran into the shop, soaked from the rain. In his hands was a small
toy car with a missing wheel.
"Can you fix it?" he asked nervously.
Mr. Arun smiled kindly. "Of course. Everything deserves another chance."
While the rain tapped on the roof, Mr. Arun carefully repaired the tiny car. When he finished, he placed
it in Rohan's hands.
Rohan's face lit up brighter than the lamp in the window.
"How much do I owe you?" the boy asked.
Mr. Arun shook his head. "Just promise me something. When you grow up, help fix something for
someone else."
Years later, Rohan became an engineer who designed machines that helped farmers work more easily.
Whenever someone thanked him, he simply smiled and said the same words he once heard in a small
shop on a rainy night:
"Everything deserves another chance."
And somewhere in that village, people still remembered the warm lamp that shined through the rain.
